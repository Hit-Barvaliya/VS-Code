Iconic Dances from of India

North India (Punjab)

⮚ North India is known for its elegant and vibrant attire. Men typically wear kurta-pajama or sherwani during formal occasions, while women prefer salwar kameez, often adorned with phulkari embroidery. The clothing reflects the region's festive spirit and diverse cultural traditions. Winters also influence the use of heavier fabrics like wool and velvet.

East India (Assam)

⮚ East India showcases handwoven and region-specific garments. Women in Assam wear the mekhela chador, while Odisha is famous for Sambalpuri and Bomkai sarees. Men usually wear dhoti-kurta with local fabric variations. Tribal influence is strong here, making the designs rich in symbolism and natural motifs.

West India (Gujrat)

⮚ Western Indian clothing is colorful and festive, influenced by desert culture and traditional crafts. Women wear ghagra choli or bandhani sarees with mirror work, while men wear kediyu, dhoti, or bandhgala. The styles are vibrant, especially during Navratri and other folk festivals. Fabrics are often breathable and suited for arid weather.

South India (Tamil Nadu)

⮚ South Indian clothing is known for its grace, simplicity, and silk tradition. Men often wear a veshti (dhoti) with an angavastram, while women wear Kanjeevaram sarees with gold borders and temple jewelry. The outfits are ideal for hot climates and religious ceremonies. The intricate weaving patterns symbolize cultural richness.

Central India

⮚ Modern Indian fashion blends traditional roots with contemporary designs. Outfits like kurti with jeans, Indo-western gowns, Nehru jackets, and designer sarees are popular among youth. This style reflects urban lifestyle and changing fashion trends. It's a celebration of tradition with a modern twist.
